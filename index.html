Capitol Hill Index

Who's in Congress, how long they've been there, and how they handle money: stock trades, PAC donations, and a financial integrity score.

How the score works

Each member gets a score from 0 to 100, where higher means cleaner. It combines three things:

  • Trading restraint. Fewer stock trades per year in office scores higher. One trade a year scores 75, three a year scores 50, and fifteen or more scores 0. Only trades since April 2012, when the STOCK Act started requiring disclosure, count.
  • Filing on time. The share of trades reported within the STOCK Act's 45-day deadline.
  • PAC independence. How little of their campaign money comes from PACs. 25% from PACs scores 50; half or more scores 0. Corporate PACs only, when that breakdown is available.

If one piece of data is missing for a member, that part is left out and the other parts make up the score. A low score means the pattern deserves a closer look. It doesn't prove anyone did something wrong.
